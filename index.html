The original question

Your supplied question. The explanation below redraws the geometry to scale.

A circle is inscribed in a triangle whose sides are 10, 10 and 12 units. A second, smaller circle is tangent to the first circle and to both equal sides. Find its radius.

Given side lengths

AB=AC=10
BC=12

Given contacts

The large circle touches all three sides. The small one touches the equal sides and the large circle.

Notation: R is the large radius, r is the unknown small radius, O′ is the large centre, and O is the small centre.

Follow the construction, one stage at a time.

Large circleSmall circleConstruction or centre line

The complete explanation

1

Given lengths → calculated height

Find the height: where the 8 comes from.

The equal sides are AB=AC=10, and the base is BC=12. These are the numbers supplied in the question.

Let F be the midpoint of the base. In an isosceles triangle, the line from the top vertex to this midpoint is perpendicular to the base and bisects the top angle. Thus:

BF=FC=122=6AF⊥BC

In right triangle ACF, the sloping side AC is the hypotenuse. Pythagoras gives:

AF2+62=102AF2=100−36=64AF=8
The height 8 is calculated, not another given. It comes from the right triangle with hypotenuse 10 and base 6.
2

Justify the construction

Why both circle centres lie on this height.

Write R for the large radius and r for the small radius. We use O′ for the large centre and O for the small centre. The points E and D are their contact points on AC.

Each centre is the same perpendicular distance from the two equal sides: that distance is its circle’s radius. A point inside an angle that is equidistant from its sides lies on the angle bisector. Therefore both centres lie on AF.

A,O,O′,F lie on one straight line
Why does equal distance from the sides imply an angle bisector?

Join a centre to the two points where its circle touches the sides. The two triangles so formed are right-angled, have equal radius legs, and share the segment from the apex to the centre as their hypotenuse. They are congruent by the right-angle–hypotenuse–side test. Their angles at the apex are therefore equal.

A radius is perpendicular to a tangent at the contact point, so:

O′E⊥ACOD⊥AC
Point names matter. D and E are on the side. O and O′ are centres. They are not interchangeable.
3

Opposite ÷ hypotenuse

Use similar triangles to describe the large radius.

Compare the full half-triangle ACF with the smaller right triangle AO′E. The right angles are at F and E. They also have the same angle at A, because AE runs along AC and AO′ runs along AF.

The triangles are similar. Compare the side opposite the shared angle with the hypotenuse:

Half of the original triangle
FCAC=610
Triangle for the large circle
O′EAO′=RAO′
RAO′=610=35

This is also the sine ratio sin⁡θ=oppositehypotenuse. No angle needs to be calculated.

Why 6 and 10? They are the opposite side and hypotenuse of triangle ACF. The height 8 is not its hypotenuse.
4

Two derived lengths

Find the large radius, then the centre distance 5.

The large circle touches the base at F, so O′F=R. The entire height is 8. The segment from the apex to the large centre is what remains after removing that bottom radius:

AO′+O′F=AFAO′+R=8AO′=8−R

Substitute this into the ratio from the previous step:

R8−R=355R=3(8−R)5R=24−3R8R=24R=3

Now calculate the other length that we will need:

AO′=8−R=8−3=5
Keep these two results separate: the large radius is 3. The distance from A to its centre is 5.
5

Unknown radius → unknown centre distance

Describe the small circle in exactly the same way.

Now compare right triangles ACF and AOD. They share the same angle at A, and their right angles are at F and D. Therefore they are similar too.

This time the opposite side is the small radius OD=r, and the hypotenuse is AO. Thus:

rAO=610=35

Rearrange the proportion without skipping the multiplication:

5r=3AOAO=5r3
This does not mean the small radius is 5/3. It means the apex-to-centre distance is five-thirds of whatever the small radius is.
6

One small radius + one large radius

Use tangency: the centre gap is the sum of the radii.

Let T be the point where the two circles touch. This is an added label, not an extra assumption. For externally tangent circles, the centres and touching point are on one straight line.

Here the order is A→O→T→O′→F. From the small centre to T is its radius r. From T to the large centre is its radius 3.

OO′=OT+TO′OO′=r+3
Why add, not subtract? The circles touch externally: travelling from one centre to the other crosses one radius of each circle. Neither circle is inside the other.

The drawing isolates the centre line so that the lengths can be read without the curved outlines getting in the way.

7

The bridge between geometry and algebra

The crucial equation: two descriptions of the same length.

We now describe the same complete segment AO′ in two different ways.

From the full triangle

The height is 8 and the lower radius is 3.

AO′=8−3=5
By splitting at the small centre

Travel from A to O, then from O to O′.

AO′=AO+OO′
AO′=5r3+(r+3)

Both expressions represent AO′, so they must be equal:

5=5r3+(r+3)
Where does the 5 come from? It is the already-calculated length AO′=8−3. It is not a new value inserted into the problem. The right side is that very same length, split into two pieces.
8

Final radius + independent arithmetic check

Solve, then check all the lengths.

Start with the equation we have just justified:

5=5r3+r+32=5r3+r2=8r36=8rr=68=34=0.75

The subtraction of 3 removes the known large radius. Multiplying by 3 clears the denominator. Dividing by 8 gives the small radius.

Exact check

AO=53×34=54OO′=3+34=154AO+OO′=54+154=204=5=AO′
The radius of the second circle is 34 unit. Its diameter would be 32 units, but the question asks for the radius.

A visual shortcut once the large radius is known.

This is an alternative check. Draw the common tangent to the circles at T. It is horizontal because the centres are vertically aligned. Let it meet the sides at G and H.

The top of the large circle is 2R=6 above the base. The full height is 8, so the little top triangle has height 8−6=2.

Triangles AGH and ABC are similar because GH∥BC. Their height ratio is 28=14. Their incircles scale by the same factor:

rR=28=14r=3×14=34
The small circle is the incircle of the top triangle: it touches both sloping sides and its new base GH. The horizontal line is a construction, not extra information from the question.

Final answer

The second circle has radius:

r=34 unit=0.75 unit

The essential connection is that AO′ is both 5 and the sum 5r3+(r+3). Equating them is what determines the unknown radius.
